# Claude Code prompt: design the analysis and visualizations

Review https://github.com/nikbearbrown/social-media-agent and its `docs/grant-pilots.md`, `agents/README.md`, and `examples/`. Propose the analysis and visualizations that should sit after collection for Humanitarians AI’s three lexicon proposals: BPHC I3-03 (disease, syndrome and symptom vocabulary), I3-04 (symptom versus intervention vocabulary), and I3-08 (ambiguous drug terminology, provisional until the sponsor brief is confirmed). Do not redesign the scraper, collect live data, change the repo, or invent findings. Use synthetic illustrations only. Treat organizational public material as evidence of language use, not of disease, substance use, or events in a population. Keep social and clinical data separate; no person-level, demographic, or fine-grained geographic inference.

For **each proposal**, give:

1. **Decision.** What would a reviewer do differently based on the analysis? Name one primary question, one unit of analysis, and what would count as evidence.
2. **Keyword analysis.** Specify the baseline and what adding reviewed aliases would change. Define the numerator and denominator for each metric, distinguish literal mentions from correct meanings, and evaluate false matches and missed meanings on a small, independently labeled fictional set. Explain how to treat duplicates, ambiguous terms, small counts, and a failed or missing source. If proposing comparisons over time, explain how to keep publisher coverage and vocabulary version comparable. Do not turn a missing feed into a zero.
3. **Charts.** Propose two charts and one reviewer table per proposal. For each, name the chart type, fields or axes, denominator and coverage note, and the decision it supports. Say why that chart is preferable to the obvious alternative. Include mock rows or chart-ready synthetic data so another person could build it. Consider a paired precision/recall or correct/incorrect comparison, a false-match breakdown, and a source-coverage chart, but choose what fits rather than using all three by default.
4. **First deliverable.** Pick the smallest decision-ready view for each grant. Identify what the current aggregate output can already support and what would require labeled examples, a revised output schema, or separately approved data. End with a prioritized implementation plan, explicitly separating a first demo from future work.

Return a concise design memo and one chart specification table. Include a separate recommendation for what the three proposals can share without creating a combined trend or overclaiming what the data measure.
